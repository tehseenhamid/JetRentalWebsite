THE VELORA JOURNAL

Charter or jet card? Start with how you fly.

Compare on-demand charter, jet cards and fractional access. Understand commitment, pricing, peak-day rules and the questions to ask each provider.

Velora Air editorial concept · Reviewed September 22, 2026

On-demand charter: evaluate each journey

On-demand charter generally involves a separate proposal and agreement for each trip. It can suit travelers who want to compare options against a specific itinerary. Availability and prices are established for the requested journey.

Jet cards: evaluate the program

A jet card typically involves prepaid hours or funds and access governed by a program agreement. Compare the aircraft category, rate basis, notice period, peak-day rules, expiry, refund conditions and the treatment of unused funds.

Fractional or lease access: evaluate the commitment

Ownership and lease arrangements are longer-term decisions. Consider the upfront commitment, recurring fees, usage charges, contract duration and exit terms. Appropriate professional advice may be useful before entering a substantial agreement.

Compare the travel you actually do

Use your expected trips as examples. A local return flight, a holiday one-way and an international sector can be treated differently. Ask every provider to explain how the same itineraries would be handled.

Look beyond the headline rate

A useful comparison includes availability terms, repositioning, taxes, minimum billing, peak restrictions and recovery when plans change. There is no universally best choice; the right fit depends on your travel and the actual contract.
